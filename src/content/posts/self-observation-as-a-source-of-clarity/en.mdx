Meditation is a state of crystalline clarity — one that arises from understanding the natural balance of being, where opposing forces coexist in harmony. I speak of a clarity that belongs to the very nature of existence, not to the human mind with its tendency toward illusion and fantasy.
<br />
Consider this: a stone falls into a stream, stirring up the silt from the riverbed and clouding the water. No matter what you try to do at that moment, the water won’t become clear again instantly. But given time, the silt settles on its own, and the natural flow restores its transparency  without any effort on your part.
<br />
People often try to become happy by relying on external aids: psychological advice, self-help techniques, affirmations, mantras, rituals, religion… These can be helpful but only temporarily. Because many of these approaches lack one essential element: self-observation. They offer no clear, steady gaze turned inward.
<br />
Take affirmations or motivational phrases, for example. They often create a split between where you are now and where you want to be. One part of the self is trying to convince or “fix” another part as if negotiating with your own shadow. But no matter how hard you try, you can’t fight your shadow into disappearing.
<br />
The secret of meditation lies in this: through quiet, consistent observation without interference or striving you begin to uncover a deep inner stillness that underlies all experiences. From this place of calm clarity, the illusions you’ve carried about yourself begin to dissolve on their own.
