Self-observation and the awareness of what is happening — this is the essence of who we are.
<br />
Attention is the only instrument through which we truly exist. When attention toward ourselves is lacking, it creates the feeling that life is unfolding in the wrong direction. Everyday circumstances are secondary. What matters most is how we relate to everything we experience.
<br />
Take a look around… When was the last time you felt genuine gratitude and smiled simply because of what you already have? Most of the time, we wander in a haze of thoughts, searching for ways to keep the wheel of samsara spinning. We chase happiness, believing it lies in material abundance, and spend years uncovering one undeniable truth: happiness is within. It is a fullness, a quiet sense of being complete,  expressed through awareness, sensations, and presence. 
<br />
Yet, not knowing how to access this state directly, we look for indirect routes — through intensity, stimulation, and constant activity. That’s why we reach for novelty: new things, new relationships, new experiences…Extreme sports, affairs, crime, drugs, alcohol — all of them are just attempts to intensify sensation. But beyond a few sharp moments, life continues in fragments, half-asleep. Because it lacks one thing: the fullness of being in touch with oneself.
<br />
Attention is simple — we just need to stop trying to fly the plane without a pilot. Whenever we fixate on something external, we forget ourselves completely and the result is the “empty house” effect, where thoughts drift like wind through open doors.
<br />
This is why we meditate: to anchor our attention within, and to realise that we don’t need to control what is happening. What we need is to learn how to experience our presence in it — to fill ourselves with awareness. To live ourselves directly, not through the filter of thought.
<br />
There’s a simple exercise. While walking, notice a row of trees then shift your attention to the space between them. Suddenly, the image changes. This little trick reveals just how focused we are on objects, and how easily we miss what matters most: the space that holds everything.
<br />
We struggle to shift from outer stimulation to inner silence. That’s why it matters to unroll the mat, cross the legs, bind the body with space and help ourselves arrive in the here and now, rather than following the next “important” thought that arises in the mind.
