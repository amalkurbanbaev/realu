Modern people often live with a hidden sense that something, as yet unexpressed, is missing from their lives. A quiet inner knowing suggests that spiritual development is real, and not merely a matter of idle fantasies or wishful thinking.
<br />
In exploring the experiences of ancient practitioners and saints, one begins to realise that there is a way to find inner harmony and lasting happiness — a path that lies in turning inward and discovering one’s true nature.
<br />
Spiritual teachers across cultures have long pointed to the same truth: the deeper meaning of self-development is not to be found outside, but within. Jesus said, “The Kingdom of God is within you.” The sages of ancient Greece taught: “Know thyself and you will know the universe and the gods.” The Indian mystic Ramakrishna told a parable of a mythical deer, tirelessly seeking the source of a fragrance that was in fact coming from within its own body. In the Bhagavad Gita, Krishna states clearly that meditation is more important than the accumulation of knowledge.
<br />
What’s needed is simply to bring a gentle focus to one’s inner state. This personal effort has the power to transform the nature of the mind — a mind whose reactions and opinions are often shaped more by instinct than by conscious choice. In this process, basic breath awareness and foundational concentration practices become powerful allies. They help regulate negative emotions rooted in the outward orientation.
